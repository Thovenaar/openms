### Presented movement smoothness

Held-key movement in the online client drew the local player at the raw 30 ms prediction state, a 33 Hz staircase with uneven spacing, while the offline client already interpolated its accumulator. Measured in real Chromium on `000010000` at 1280×900, sampling the presented pose on every animation frame while one real key was held, with [`client/tools/movement-smoothness.js`](../client/tools/movement-smoothness.js) (`bun run smoothness`):

| Run                                                     | Stalled frames | Jerk |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------: | ---: |
| Offline client, held ArrowRight                         |          0.000 | 0.087 |
| Offline client, held Alt (repeated jumps)                |          0.000 | 0.193 |
| Online before the fix, held ArrowRight (whole window)    |          0.619 | 1.628 |
| Online after the fix, held ArrowRight                    |          0.007 | 0.130 |
| Online after the fix, held Alt (repeated jumps)          |          0.007 | 0.171 |
| Same-trace raw-kernel control, offline / online          |   0.448 / 0.453 | 1.621 / 1.608 |

A stalled frame is one whose presented per-frame displacement magnitude (both axes) is ≤0.02 px while the authoritative kernel advanced more than 2 px within the trailing 100 ms, so deceleration, terrain blocks and post-release frames are excluded; jerk is mean |Δd − Δd_prev| / mean |d| over those magnitudes. The raw-kernel control replays the previous presentation from the same trace (both series share one gate), which is why it reproduces the pre-fix regime without a second run; the pre-fix row above used the tool's earlier whole-window metric, before the gate existed.

The fix is presentation-only: `OnlinePrediction.interpolate(now, target)` presents the newest two authenticated 30 ms kernel states anchored to the scheduler's actual step time, and the fixed predictor scheduler polls every 4 ms so each step lands within one 4 ms period of its tick boundary instead of within 10 ms. Simulation state, checkpoint restore/replay and transmitted input are untouched — prediction reported zero corrections, zero position error and zero resync overflows across all measured runs. Two regressions in [sync-alignment.test.js](../client/test/sync-alignment.test.js) pin the contract at the predictor boundary: the presented pose stays inside the newest two kernel states without extrapolating past either end, and interpolation never writes back into the kernel (both fail against a build that writes the presented pose into the simulation). Retained traces: [offline walk](validation/online-movement/offline/report.json), [offline jumps](validation/online-movement/offline-jump/report.json), [online walk](validation/online-movement/online/report.json) and [online jumps](validation/online-movement/online-jump/report.json), each with its `frames.json` step trace.

Not claimed here: the camera is VR-clamped on `000010000` at this viewport, so its metrics are uninformative and follow smoothness on large maps is not separately measured; peers, mobs and drops still chase the server's 90 ms entity publications, which this change does not alter; and no frame-rate or original-Windows-parity claim follows from these ratios.
